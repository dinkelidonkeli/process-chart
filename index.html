Four truths about how a business works

Each one builds on the one before: from processes, to the people and applications that run them, to the data they leave behind.

  1. 1

    Every business can be described as a network of connected processes.

    Processes describe how the business works. The business itself consists of people, assets, information, legal entities and culture.

    It lives through its relationships with customers, suppliers and partners.

  2. 2

    Processes fall into three layers.

    Management processes steer the business, core processes create and deliver value to customers, and support processes enable both. Some domains, like asset management, serve more than one layer.

  3. 3

    Processes are carried out by people and applications, including connected machines and AI.

    Every step reads, creates or changes data. Almost everything a business owns or does, from a screw in the warehouse to an hour worked, is represented as data somewhere.

  4. 4

    Data should be the truth about the company’s situation.

    In practice, it’s only as accurate as the processes, people and applications that create it.

        Things to keep in mind

        • Microsoft doesn’t map its catalog to APQC. The placement of Microsoft process areas into domains here is a judgment call.
        • Microsoft’s export has no stable unique ID, so use its process sequence numbers with care.
        • Order to cash in Microsoft covers selling and getting paid. Shipping the goods sits in Inventory to deliver.
        • APQC 8.0 renamed and added elements compared with 7.4. Hierarchy numbers can change between versions while PCF IDs stay the same.
